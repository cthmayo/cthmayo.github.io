  <h2>Research</h2>

  <p>
    My research focuses on the development and evaluation of machine learning
    and statistical methods for healthcare. My main interests include
    multimodal machine learning, clinical risk prediction, causal machine learning,
    and physiological signal and medical image processing. I have also been the lead
    biostatistician on a variety of medical research projects in fields ranging from
    diabetes pharmacoepidemiology and orthopaedic surgery. 
  </p>

  <hr />

  <h3>Theses and dissertations</h3>

  <h4>Machine Learning for Nociception Assessment from Single-Channel EEG</h4>
  <p>
    My MSc research investigated methods for detecting and quantifying
    nociception during general anaesthesia using single-channel
    electroencephalography. The project included EEG signal processing,
    unsupervised machine learning, supervised machine learning, and the development
    of a novel nonlinear mixed-effects model combining machine learning with
    pharmacodynamic modelling techniques.
  </p>

  <h4>Causal Machine Learning for Advanced Recovery Room Care</h4>
  <p>
    This dissertation applied conditional average treatment-effect estimators
    to post-anaesthetic recovery data. I used explainability methods to
    characterise treatment-effect heterogeneity and investigate how these models
    could inform patient selection for Advanced Recovery Room Care. Findings
    from the wider project were presented at the 2025 ANZCA Annual Scientific
    Meeting.
  </p>

  <h4>Multimodal Machine Learning for Glaucoma Risk Prediction</h4>
  <p>
    My medical elective project developed a multimodal machine-learning model
    for predicting the risk of primary open-angle glaucoma. This work was
    completed as an honours-equivalent dissertation at the University of
    Auckland and presented at the 2022 Glaucoma New Zealand Professional
    Symposium.
  </p>

  <hr />

  <h3>Clinical biostatistics and health-data research</h3>

  <p>
    I have contributed statistical analyses to research in diabetes,
    orthopaedic surgery, medication use, and health-service delivery. This work
    has included data cleaning and linkage, regression and mixed-effects
    modelling, algorithm evaluation, longitudinal analysis, and the preparation
    of journal manuscripts, reports, conference presentations, and posters.
  </p>
  <hr />

  <h3>Other applied projects</h3>

  <h4>Prediction of Multidrug-Resistant Organisms</h4>
  <p>
    At the 2023 Hack Aotearoa healthcare datathon, I contributed to a project
    developing models to predict multidrug-resistant organisms from clinical
    data. The project received the awards for Best Overall and Most
    Reproducible.
  </p>

  <h4>Cardiovascular and Cortical Responses to Peritoneal Insufflation</h4>
  <p>
    This project, which I undertook for my medical selective,
    involved processing and analysing EEG and physiological data,
    visualising multidimensional signals, and developing a mixed-effects
    explanatory model to investigate responses during surgery.
  </p>

  <h4>Australian Diabetes Data Network</h4>
  <p>
    I contributed to establishing the first New Zealand adult site for the
    Australian Diabetes Data Network. The project included clinical-data
    cleaning and analysis, as well as evaluation of algorithms for
    differentiating type 1 and type 2 diabetes using routinely collected data.
  </p>
